How These Numbers Are Calculated

PM compliance — a preventative WO counts as on time when it is completed by 6:00 am on the Monday after its due date (the "due week" rule). Completed later counts as late.

Proactive / planned work — preventative, corrective, service and project orders. Reactive — breakdown orders. Shop, request and production orders are support work and sit outside both.

Breakdown rate — breakdown WOs as a share of all completed WOs in the period. Planned : BD hours — labor on planned work divided by labor on breakdowns.

Schedule compliance — WOs on the A/B/C/D shift schedules with a scheduled date inside the Mon–Sun week that appear in the completed report (or are checked off manually). Mezz is tracked separately.

Correctives over 30 days — open correctives whose requested date is more than 30 days ago. Overdue — open correctives past their due date.

Repeat work — a breakdown or corrective on an asset that had another unplanned order 1–30 days earlier; same-day pairs are ignored. Used in place of a rework count, which PMware does not record.

Cause named — share of breakdown WOs whose title says what failed (read from keywords; "Not specified" and bare "down / not running" titles don't count). FMEA, Pareto and Weibull by cause all depend on it.

MTBF / MTTR — from PMware's Metrics › MTBF\MTTR page for the IG lines. MTBF = line hours ÷ breakdowns, MTTR = repair time ÷ breakdowns; availability = line hours less repair time. One page per line per month; combining months or lines applies the same formulas to the combined totals.

Estimate variance — booked labor against the estimate, only on WOs that have both. Labor hours are what was entered on the work order, not payroll time.

Source — PMware completed-WO report, corrective W/O list and shift schedule pages. Per-person views list everyone who closed work orders.

Analytics

Tools for deciding what to fix: where breakdown time goes, how each machine fails, which PM estimates are wrong, and which PMs actually prevent breakdowns. Uses the year, month, department and work-center filters.

Pareto
Ranked list red rank = the vital few that make up the first 80%
How to use it

A Pareto ranks problems biggest first, with a running total. Usually a handful of items make up most of the total — the vital few. Work on those first: pull their work orders, find what physically failed and fix the cause instead of repairing it again. Group by Failure cause to see which kind of problem costs most plant-wide, or by Equipment + cause to get specific root-cause targets. Causes are read from keywords in the WO title, so "Not specified" means the title didn't say what failed. Labor that looks mistyped (over 24 h, or 8 h+ and 8× the estimate) is left out.

Failure pattern by machine β with 90% range · dashed line = 1
How to read it

Each machine with 8 or more breakdowns gets a Weibull fit from the days between its breakdowns.

β below 1 — early failures. Breakdowns cluster right after a repair: the fix isn't holding. Look at repair quality, parts and root cause; more PM won't help.

β near 1 — random. Breakdowns come at any time. A PM can't be timed to beat them; change what the PM checks, use inspections, and fix the top causes on the Pareto.

β above 1 — wear-out. Risk grows with time since the last repair. Time-based PM works: service before the B10 day, when 10% would have failed.

η is the typical days between breakdowns (63% have happened by then). Trend says whether breakdowns are coming closer together through the period. A machine only counts as early failure or wear-out when its whole 90% range is on one side of 1.

How often they break down vs. how often they get PM

η is roughly how many days a machine runs between breakdowns. The PM interval is the typical gap between its PM work orders. A machine that breaks down several times between PMs is a sign the PM isn't covering what fails — or isn't happening often enough.

Weibull by equipment click a column to sort
Breakdowns right after PM share in the first third of the cycle between PMs
How to read it

For every breakdown that happens between two PMs on the same machine, this checks where in that PM cycle it fell. If the PM made no difference, about a third would land in the first third of the cycle (the dashed line).

Working — clearly fewer breakdowns right after the PM. The PM is holding failures off; breakdowns build up later in the cycle. Keep it, and don't stretch the interval.

No effect — breakdowns land anywhere in the cycle. The PM isn't checking what actually fails. Rewrite its task list around the machine's top breakdown cause (see Pareto), or stretch the interval and use the hours elsewhere.

Breakdowns after PM — clearly more breakdowns right after the PM. Something about the PM is disturbing the machine: reassembly, adjustments, settings. Add a run check at the end of the PM.

Machines need 8+ breakdowns that fall between two PMs. The test uses 90% confidence, so with many machines a few verdicts will be chance — check the work orders before changing a PM.

How it works

Actual time is the median labor on completions where real time was entered. Labor that exactly equals the estimate is left out — it is almost always the default accepted, not a timed job. A change is suggested when actual differs from the estimate by more than 25% and at least 10 minutes, rounded to 5 minutes (15 above an hour). Confidence: High = 8+ timed completions with consistent times; Low = few completions or times vary a lot — ask the tech before changing. Labor is total crew time, so a two-person PM shows both people's time.

Save any of these pages from PMWare as Webpage, Complete or HTML only and drop them in (several at once is fine):

Completed Work Orders report — pmw: w/o report — rebuilds every KPI (PM on-time, breakdowns, labor, technicians). Rows merge by WO#, so dropping a new month-to-date report just adds the new ones.
Corrective W/O list — pmw: w/o list — replaces the open-correctives snapshot.
MTBF\MTTR page — pmw: MTBF\MTTR — Reports › Metrics › MTBF\MTTR, one page per IG line per month (From/To = the month). Name the file with the month, e.g. MTBF IG1 2026-10.html; the same line and month dropped again replaces the earlier page.
Shift schedules — pmw: schedule — one file per shift (A/B/C/D/Mezz). Each drop replaces that shift's plan. Completion is detected automatically when the WO# appears in a completed-WO report or the schedule page shows it as Completed; use the checkbox for manual overrides. The combined All schedule page is split back into shifts using the single-shift pages you have loaded before.
